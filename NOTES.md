# NOTES

## camber hulls (branch `camber-sweep`, worktree `~/projects/HydroSandbox-camber`)

Started 2026-11-14. camber (github.com/electricshipwrights/camber, TypeScript) is
ported to Python, not called through boatmath's Rust importer (Avi's call).

Done:
- `hydrosandbox/hull/camber/`: document reader (v1 via camber's converter, v2),
  curve primitives (C2 PCHIP, clamped B-spline plan, knuckled Catmull-Rom), the
  sweep and its trims (camber's regularized `sweptSection` path), and family
  promote + blend. Matches camber to round-off on 4 test hulls and 3 families
  (`tests/camber/golden.json`, generated by `tests/camber/make_golden.mjs` from a
  camber checkout; see its header). camber has no license file, so its own example
  hulls are not committed; the test hulls were written for this.
- `Hull.from_camber`: sweep -> starboard mesh -> offsets over the hull's own
  extent. Displacement within 0.6% and LCB within 0.5% L of camber's hydrostatics.
- `Hull.from_mesh(level_oversampling=n)`: each level's half-breadth averaged over
  its band (hat-weighted). Without it, offsets jump as a flat bottom / chine /
  raked transom crosses a level, and Rw jumped ~7% between neighbouring blends.
  Mesh cutting is vectorized over levels.
- `HullFamily`/`BlendedHull`: exact blends at a simplex lattice (5 nodes for 2
  hulls), interpolated by Lagrange polynomials in the weights. Everything a Hull
  computes (incl. attitude tables, sections, keel height) is mixed; weights may be
  CasADi variables. Off-node error vs the exact blend: volume <0.5%, Rw <1%.
- `optimize()` in `optimize_foils_on_hull.py` takes a HullFamily (vars `blend_t` or
  `blend_w*`) and re-evaluates the solution on the exact blend.
  `examples/optimize_camber_blend.py` is the CLI.

Findings (pre-existing Hull behaviour, not changed):
- Hull's offset-table wetted surface is 5-8% above the true swept area on these
  chined test hulls (swept mesh matches camber to 0.1%), so friction is high by
  the same.
- Rw on the test family at Fn 0.2 is not grid-converged: 164 / 189 / 207 N at
  41 / 81 / 161 stations.

`local/camber_blend_study.json`; inputs ~/Cal 20.json, ~/NPish ... v2.json):
- Both scaled to LOA 6.11 m, sections promoted on-curve, mass fixed at the Cal 20
  canoe body's 1224 kg, CG at each blend's level-at-rest LCB, 21 exact blends x
  2.0 / 2.75 / 3.25 m/s.
- Thin-ship is out of its validity range here (L/B ~2.7-3): a Wigley of the same
  L and displacement gives the same magnitudes (233 N at 2 m/s, 3.1 kN at 3.25),
  several times a real Cal 20's. Treat as a ranking at best; there are also jumps
  in D(t) at 2.75 m/s (t 0.55-0.60, 0.70-0.75) not yet explained.
- Interpolant check on the test family: optimizer from t=0.1 found 0.067 (exact
  optimum ~0.2, 2% worse); from t=0.8 a spurious minimum at 0.735. For 1-D blends
  a scan of exact blends is the reliable answer.
- Avi isn't interested in the Delft series; testing with narrow multihulls instead.

Narrow catamaran study (`local/camber_cat_study.py` / `.json`): both shapes at
LOA 6.11 m squeezed (`scaled(beam_factor=)`) to underwater L/B 12, spacing 2.5 m,
total mass 664 kg (two narrow Cal 20s), CG level at rest, Fn 0.30/0.45/0.60.
Magnitudes sensible (R/W ~1.3% / 4.5% / 6.5%). Optimum blend: t~0.05 at Fn 0.3
(Cal-ish), t~0.40 at Fn 0.45 and 0.6 (14% / 10% below the Cal 20 shape, both

workers): t* = 0.034 / 0.37 / 0.406 at Fn 0.30 / 0.45 / 0.60, exact-blend drag
within 0.2 N of the scan's best at every speed from both starts.

Pi foil + blend on the same cat (`local/camber_cat_foils.py` / `.json`, 36
solves, 3 starting layouts, free blend from t=0.4 plus fixed exact blends t =
0/0.2/0.4/0.6/1.0, trim bounds -2..4 deg, no GM_L floor, peak ~6.4 GB):
- Fn 0.45: t* = 0.48, D 197 N (wave 49, viscous 126, foil 21), foil carries 16%
  of weight. Fixed t: 249 / 211 / 196 / 205 / 285 N at t = 0 / .2 / .4 / .6 / 1.
- Fn 0.60: t* = 0.42, D 347 N (wave 112, viscous 211, foil 23), 15% lift.
  Fixed: 399 / 360 / 347 / 355 / failed (t=1, all starts).
- All 3 starts agree for the free blend. Two fixed-t runs from layout 2 found a
  worse local optimum (foil forward, -3 deg incidence, ~100 N worse).
- Blend optimum barely moves with foils (0.37->0.48, 0.41->0.42), and the free
  run's t matches the fixed-t scan's best within its resolution.
- Every solution has trim at the 4 deg bound and the main foil at its aft bound
  (x_bow + 0.95 L): the foil is used as a trim tab. Bare hull forced to fixed

  So of the 292->197 N gain at Fn 0.45, ~45 N is trim alone and ~50 N comes
  from the foil (unloading plus wave interference: wave 109 -> 49 N). Thin-ship
  at 4 deg trim on a 6 m hull is a stretch; treat the trim gain as optimistic.

## hydrosandbox (branch `hydrosandbox`)

Gemini-authored hydrofoil package on top of AeroSandbox; reviewed 2026-11-14.

Done:
- Baseline import committed unmodified, then three review fixes:
  - `free_surface_induced_drag_factor` / `free_surface_lift_slope_factor` now use
    Prandtl's biplane factor with gap = 2h (matches HydroVLM within ~0.04).
  - HydroVLM `panel_Cpmin` is derived from strip-integrated section Cl
    (mesh-convergent) instead of leading-edge panel loading.
  - HydroVLM panel t/c no longer uses an `id()`-keyed cache (stale hits across foils).
- Regression tests added for all three; example STEP regenerated.

- Wave-making drag added to all three solvers (`library/wave_resistance.py`,
  `hydrodynamics/wave_drag.py`), ported from boatmath's `thinship` Michell
  conventions into differentiable Python (boatmath is Rust; not a dependency).
  - Sources for thickness (struts = Michell thin ship, foils, pods), dipoles for
    lift and side force, one spectrum so interference is included.
  - Drag due to lift = rigid-lid-image vortex drag + R_w. R_w -> 2x the phi=0
    image mutual drag as Fr -> inf, so it must not be added on top of the
    high-Froude image induced drag (the solvers swap the image term out).
  - Validated: Wigley closed form (0.13%), 2D vortex (<0.5%), infinite-Froude
    image limit (0.02% kernel, 0.4% through VLM/LL).

- Follow-up review fixes, merged from branch `hydrosandbox-fixes`:
  - `wing_thickness_wave_sources` now interpolates chord and thickness jumps
    onto the submerged `[t_lo, t_hi]` sub-interval (fixing tapered piercing struts).
  - `kelvin_wave_resistance` infers quadrature scales from any numeric
    coordinates in partially symbolic `Opti` graphs. (A symbolic `velocity`
    still requires `reference_velocity`: the branch's 5 m/s default was dropped
    on merge.)
  - `HydroBuildup._build_wave_lift_elements` projects elliptical weights onto
    vertical (`|u_z|`) vs lateral (`|u_y|`) lifting spans so multi-section
    winglet / C-foil tips do not mix vertical lift and tip sideforce.
  - Fixed `junction_interference_drag` exponent to Hoerner Ch. 8 Eq. 2
    (`/ tc**2`) and re-exported wave-resistance and biplane helpers at top level.

- Hull in the loop (`hydrosandbox/hull/`): port of boatmath (`hullgeom`,
  `thinship`) to differentiable Python.
  - `Hull`: offsets table (from numbers, mesh, or boatmath hull-record geometry);
    hydrostatics, ITTC-57, thin-ship sources, transom closure, and the dynamic
    sinkage force / trim moment (`thinship::squat`) at any sinkage and trim.
  - `CraftBuildup`: hull + foil assembly as one rigid craft; shared wave
    spectrum (interference); `solve_equilibrium` (Newton, 5-15 s) or Opti
    constraints (limited-memory Hessian; start from the Newton attitude).
  - Validated against boatmath's Rust output. Wigley: Rw 0.6%, Rv 0.05%,
    dynamic force 0.3%, moment 0.5%. Real hull (`~/hummingbird.igs`, cut by
    `boatmath hull`, free attitude, Fn 0.3-0.9): sinkage 0.2 mm, trim 0.06 deg,
    Rt 1.5%. References came from a `rust:1-slim` container (no Rust toolchain
    on this box); the built CLI and outputs were in the session scratchpad and
    are not kept. The IGES and its hull record are not committed.
  - Bugs found on the way, now tested: the transom hollow must stay level
    (not pitch with trim); transom depth uses the widest *immersed* breadth;
    CasADi reshape is column-major.

- Static pitch stability: `CraftBuildup.run_with_stability()` gives heave-free

## Validation against tank data (branch `validation`, worktree `.worktrees/validation`; 2026-11-15)

`validation/` (see its README): Wigley fixed (Ju 1983), R/V Athena fixed and free
(Jenkins 1984), Southampton NPL catamaran series (Report 71, all ten models,
monohull + four spacings; tables OCR'd and checked). Another session works on
the same series' transom closure in `HydroSandbox-camber/local/npl`; the NPL body
plan here is copied from its extraction.

Findings so far:
- Wigley, fixed: Michell C_W is ~2x measured below Fn 0.25, ~1.4x at 0.25-0.32,
  within ~25% at 0.32-0.40.
- Athena (B/T 4.2), fixed: C_W / C_WP grows 1.1 (Fn 0.41) -> 1.5 (0.52) -> 2.4
  (0.8) -> 4 (1.0). `auto` closure is 5-11x high at Fn 0.28. Trim within ~20%.
- Southampton at the *measured* attitude, Fn >= 0.3: thin ship is off by a
  typical factor of 2 (closure `none` underpredicts, median 0.55; opposite sign
  to Athena).
- Spectrum-gain test (`southampton/spectrum_gain.py`): a per-demihull energy gain
  G2(theta; Fn, L/vol^(1/3), B/T), fitted with one model held out, predicts the
  held-out model's monohull C_WP to a factor 1.15 and its catamaran C_WP at all
  four spacings to 1.18 (closure `none`). Scaling each catamaran point by its own
  monohull's measured ratio gives 1.25. The fitted gain values themselves are not
  physical (poorly identified from integrated data); identical side-by-side hulls
  cannot test phase; all ten hulls are one parent form.
- The rebuilt NPL hulls' wetted surface is ~5% above Report 71's Table II.

Left:
- Free-attitude sweep done (100 curves, `southampton/report/summary.md`). Fn >= 0.65:
  C_T within ~10% with `auto` (0.90-1.01 monohull), 10-13% low with `none`. Fn 0.2-0.35:
  `auto` gives 10-30x the measured C_WP and C_T 1.1-2.1x high; `none` is 25-35% low on
  C_T. Monohull trim within ~0.15 deg for models 5 and 6, 0.4-0.5 deg low for model 4
  and 1.8 deg low for 3b above Fn 0.65. Interference factor tau: `none` matches the
  measured median at every spacing (rms 0.13-0.30); `auto` is ~0.25 high.
  Measured (C_T - C_WP) / C_F is 1.26-1.54 and the model's friction + transom drag
  reproduces it within ~0.08 with no form factor.
- Insel's 1990 thesis (`local/refs/457354.pdf`) has one tabulated measured spectrum
  (Wigley, Fn 0.331; `wigley/insel_spectrum.py`): Michell's total is 5% high and its
  transverse and cusp waves match (0.99-1.13); the diverging waves do not (1.2-1.3x at
  41-53 deg, 0.58x at 53-61 deg). The thesis's other spectra (Fn 0.5, models C2-C5)
  and all its resistance data are plots. Insel also predicted catamaran C_WP by
  superposing *measured* monohull spectra: it works but puts the hump at a shifted
  Froude number, which he puts down to hull-hull flow the superposition leaves out.
- Insel Fig. 159 digitised (`wigley/insel_fig159.py`; Wigley fixed, Fn 0.5, by tank
  harmonic): measured monohull harmonics x the linear placement factor reproduce the
  measured catamaran harmonics at four spacings (rms 0.025 in the figure's units; thin
  ship 0.070). The figure's normalisation is inferred, see the CSV header.
- Couser's 1996 thesis (`local/refs/199075.pdf`): his "slender body" method is the same
  centreplane-source thin ship, with measured trim/sinkage and a virtual appendage
  6 transom half-breadths long (tuned with Fn, his Fig. 5.8). In this code (`spectra.py`
  closures `c6`, `couser`; one length from the DWL transom half-breadth, smoothstep
  shape, not his per-waterline taper) it is 1.4-1.5x high on C_WP but consistent:
  error factor ~1.5 raw (auto 2.2, none 1.9-2.0), 1.19 after scaling by the hull's own
  monohull ratio, 1.12 / 1.15 (mono / cat) with the held-out theta gain.
  Shorter appendages are worse, not better (2, 3, 4 half-breadths: 1.8x, 1.7x, 1.6x
  high), so the unbiased length lies between 6 half-breadths and `auto`'s half
  wavelength; the camber session's wavelength-fraction scan covers that range.
- Dynamic force and moment (`southampton/attitude.py`): the measured attitude gives the
  measured dynamic force and moment (minus the hydrostatic imbalance there). Thin ship
  has the vertical force about right with a closure (-10 to -14% of weight) but its
  trim moment barely varies with fullness (38-57 against measured 43-102, in 1e-3 W L,
  Fn >= 0.65): 2.5x low on 3b. A held-out linear correction cuts force rms 3.2 -> 1.6
  (% W) and moment 19 -> 13 (closure c6).
- Surrogate training plan (Claude doc, Avi's notes in it, 2026-11-16):
  https://claude.ai/artifact/NxuucDnzpkN3MdFmF5wQF7 . Avi: cloud CFD is fine; focus on
  power-boat demihulls first; consider correcting sinkage/trim first (plan now does);
  closure change goes to the camber session.
- Phase A (`southampton/phase_a.py`, closure c6): additive ridge correction to the
  dynamic force and moment, tested closed loop with each hull held out (1700 free
  solves, ~50 min on 18 cores). Trim rms 0.41 -> 0.13 deg monohull, 0.52 -> 0.22 cat;
  sinkage 2.0 -> 0.5 and 2.1 -> 0.9 % of draught. Gate (0.3 deg, 1%) met overall; 3b
  (0.38 deg, 1.1%) and 4a (no gain) are the weak hulls. Total resistance unchanged.
  The correction is closure-specific and has only seen the Southampton family.
- Phase A generalisation (`southampton/generalisation.py`, open loop): holding out a
  whole slenderness or B/T group still works (3b moment error 44.7 -> 9.7). It does
  NOT extrapolate in speed (Fn >= 0.8 held out: force 3.3 -> 9.8), from monohulls to
  catamarans (moment 18.9 -> 32.4), or to another family: on Athena (B/T 4.1) the
  Report-71 correction makes both worse (force 6.3 -> 11.7, moment 12.3 -> 17.1), where
  plain thin ship is decent. It needs an envelope guard and more families before use
  on other hulls.
- Where Avi's hulls sit against the Report 71 envelope (NACRA demihull at 520 kg, four
  camber-generated demihulls at 500 kg; 2.2 m spacing, 3-6 m/s): inside on

- 2D+t dynamic lift tested against thin ship's shortfall (`validation/lift_2dt/`;
  boatmath's `lift.rs` formulation with Lewis-form added mass, at the measured
  attitudes, closure c6, Fn >= 0.4). Moment: right sign and trend with fullness on
  the NPL series and Series 64 but about half the size needed (slope 1.8 and 2.2,
  r ~0.6; rms 20 -> 15 and 19 -> 13); no relation on Athena (14 -> 21). Force: it adds

  64 3.6 -> 6.9, where the shortfall is *more suction*, r -0.9). Dropping the transom
  fade or the cross-flow drag changes little; the "run" angle is worse for moment.
  So it is not the missing physics in this form. Avi agreed (2026-11-16) that a
  single-family CFD surrogate is not worth training; plan doc now leads with a CFD
  spot check.
- Bow wave (2026-11-16). `athena/wave_profile.py`: thin ship's wave profile along the
  hull against Jenkins Table 8 (Athena held fixed). Bow crest height, thin / measured:
  0.98 at Fn 0.28, 0.77, 0.72, 0.61, 0.46 at Fn 0.65, and the thin-ship crest sits
  1-3 stations too far aft; thin ship's stern trough is also too deep. Noblesse et
  al.'s analytical relations (J. Hydrodynamics 25(4) 2013, eq. 6a,b; wedge form) give
  the crest position within about a station and a height a steady 1.5-1.6x the
  measured one at all five speeds (the hull is flared; their flare factors are in
  tables not in hand). But a bow-wave load scale built from them
  (`bow_wave/test.py`) does not explain thin ship's force / moment shortfall: r = 0.03
  with the moment shortfall on the NPL series (the scale hardly varies with fullness,
  the shortfall runs -15 to +51), and the fitted slopes differ in sign between
  families. So: thin ship does lack the bow wave, the relations would fix the picture,
  and neither this nor 2D+t lift accounts for the missing trim moment.
